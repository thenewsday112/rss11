Voice cloning scams: How to protect yourself from audio deepfakes

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Your phone rings, and without thinking, you answer it. It's your spouse. Or your grandma. Maybe the friend you're always posting TikToks with online. And they're asking for your help. 

They've been in a car accident, and you have to provide some personal information as their emergency contact. They're in jail and need you to bail them out. Or maybe it's even simpler than that: Grandma can't log into her account and needs to send you a code. Can you please read it to her? 

The familiar lilt of their voices catch you off guard — only none of them are real. 

Their voices have been cloned, and malicious actors are donning their audio likenesses to scam you out of your personal information, and sometimes cold, hard cash. That number is growing, making even more people susceptible to the deceptive tech, but there are very simple ways to protect yourself and your loved ones.  

Scroll through TikTok, and you'll come across hundreds of stories detailing tales concocted by scammers to impersonate your loved ones. In one such video with more than 450,000 views, a young woman writes that her mother was nearly scammed by an imposter who imitated her grandmother's voice. The only way they caught it? Grandma is no longer alive. 

Scams are on the rise. In 2025, Americans lost around $148.2 billion to digital crimes, a jump of more than 25 percent from the year before. Recent data from digital security company NordVPN found that a growing number of scammers are pitching voice scam services to bad actors on dark web forums and Telegram channels. Sales pitches for voice cloning services rose sevenfold in the last two years, reported NordVPN.

Why more people are getting fooled by audio scams 

One reason that number keeps ticking up is the proliferation of sophisticated — but more importantly accessible — generative AI tools, explained Marijus Briedis, CTO at NordVPN. 

"Some years ago, a cloned voice had something slightly off about it, and most people could hear it. That is gone now," Briedis said. 

Advanced technology is only one part of the growing scam industry, however. Briedis explained that such tools are being increasingly packaged, priced, and sold directly to the Average Joe. 

Voice cloning tools, previously a standalone novelty tech, are now sold as a single feature in all-encompassing, common criminal toolkits, or "scam kits," experts told Mashable. These kits include access to legitimate generative AI tools via jailbroken accounts, troves of personal data, and other features sold at increasingly affordable prices. 

Credit: Cole Kan / Mashable / Getty

For example, a recent study of dark web marketplaces conducted by NordVPN found that a hacked ElevenLabs Scale account — complete with unlimited voice cloning — sold for just $150. An ElevenLabs API key was going for a mere $8.

Scam networks no longer need technologically savvy hackers or technologists. Just about anyone with a computer can do it. And that means you have to be prepared. 

Step 1: Know the threat and make a plan 

"When anyone can run the attack, volume becomes the strategy," Briedis said. "No single attempt has to be perfect. There just have to be a lot of them."

But asking someone to be on alert all the time is unfair, Briedis said. Up until now, the mantra has been: "Every message, every video, every call. Get it wrong once and that is the one that counts," Briedis explained. "Nobody holds that standard across a lifetime, and most security advice assumes they will."

As an alternative, users can prepare themselves, and their community, to be able to step up when the moment calls. 

Credit: Cole Kan/Mashable/Getty

Prepare your contacts

Eyal Elazar, head of market Intelligence for online merchant security business Riskified, described effective scam preparation as being a "digital ambassador" for your inner circle. 

It begins with a little research. Find and save verified contact information for important callers, such as your bank, insurance company, or family lawyers. You can use this to cross reference callers later.

Then, talk to your circles about what a legitimate phone call would look like. Know who lists you as an emergency contact, and in what contexts. Are you a beneficiary on an account, and, if so, what do you need to know?

Set a code word

Most experts Mashable spoke to recommend creating a code word that you can use with friends and family to confirm who they are. Don't share this anywhere online. "Sounds silly right up until the day it does not," Briedis said.

Filter out the red flags  

There are also common scam requests that are instant red flags, experts told Mashable. Any demand for money over the phone is suspicious. There are more specific tells, too: 

  • Requests to read a code, sent via text or email, over the phone

  • Requests for passwords or other log-in credentials 

  • Demands for payment via gift cards, wire transfers, crypto exchanges, and payment apps

  • Suggestions to initiate a wire transfer over a voice note, a phone call, or a voicemail

  • Demands of ransom

  • Law enforcement dealings conducted via phone call

Step 2: Putting down the phone can stop a scammer.

"AI isn’t inventing new scams, but it's removing the tells we’ve learned to look for in the old ones," said Donnie Hasseltine, chief information security officer at Arkstone Defense. 

It can be hard to discern the small audio tells that make your loved ones' voice truly theirs, especially when an individual is up against social engineering that forces urgency and stirs panic. 

So, the next piece of advice is to hang up on your friends or "friends." 

Yes, this may cause some drama in the group chat. But if you can't always tell when a voice is real or cloned, it's best to cut them off and reset. 

If someone you personally know was supposedly on the other line, call them back on the number you're used to before agreeing to anything. If it's a bank or your workplace, call a listed number on an official website, the back of a credit card, or a piece of mail. Many institutions are already keenly aware of common phone scams that target customers. 

"Stop judging the content and change the channel instead," Breidis urged.

Step 3: Slow down.

The last, and most simple, piece of advice is to take a breath. Experts told Mashable that urgency is the number one red flag that may indicate you are the target of a scam, e.g., someone claiming to be from the electricity company demanding money immediately in exchange for keeping your power on. That supposed urgency also sets you up to be more susceptible to scammers. 

Andrew Jones, co-founder of Adaptive Security, said that a manufactured sense of urgency as well as a demand for secrecy are the two factors found in nearly every successful scam he has studied. 

"Nothing is so urgent that it can’t wait for you to verify it," Hasseltine agreed. 

So don't give in to the rush. Slow down, hang up the phone, and ask someone else to weigh in. 

And it bears repeating: Never. Send. Cash.

Already been scammed? Here's what to do.

If a scammer has already gotten hold of your personal information or money, act fast:

  1. Block the scammer so they can't reach out again.

  2. Cut off their access to your bank account or payment method, if you provided that information. Call your bank immediately. If you sent a wire transfer or gift card, contact the issuer and tell the company you have been scammed. Change your passwords. Freeze your credit.

  3. Report your stolen information. Go to IdentityTheft.gov to create a government-recommended recovery plan. Report the scam to the Social Security Administration’s fraud hotline if you handed over your SSN.

  4. Stay calm. The vast majority of Americans have been scammed in one way or another. There is help out there, and you can prepare your defenses for next time.

Copperfield! The New Musical review: A rollicking, big-hearted Dickens adaptation that is not to be missed

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Originally published as a serial, starting in 1849, Charles Dickens' The Personal History, Adventures, Experience and Observation of David Copperfield the Younger of Blunderstone Rookery is a tale rife with tragedy. But recent adaptations like Armando Iannucci's 2019 movie The Personal History of David Copperfield and Jeffrey Scharf's Copperfield! The New Musical focus on the hope and happiness found in a life story rocked repeatedly by woe.

However, for those taking notes on such things, Copperfield! does hew more closely to the events of Dickens' story, embracing some of its darkest moments without reserve. And yet, this new musical, now playing Off-Broadway, is a rapturous delight — perhaps in part because it doesn't shy away from its hero's hardships.

Copperfield! A New Musical is a classic story told with a raucous and sensationally talented ensemble.

Amelia Cormack, Jed Feder, Alexander Sovronsky, Henry Witcher, Cathryn Wake, and Nancy O'Connor in "Copperfield! The New Musical." Credit: Jeremy Daniel

With book, music, and lyrics by Scharf, Copperfield! is set across England, from 1835 to 1870. Directed and choreographed by Gabriel Barre (with associate director and choreographer Ashley Wren Collins), this musical kicks off with a grown David Copperfield (Bobby Conte) reflecting on his life. A dashing man, he ushers audiences back to the day of his birth, introducing his widowed mother Clara (Cathryn Wake) in fervent labor, while her devoted maid Peggotty (Amelia Cormack) assists, and his eccentric aunt Betsey Trotwood (Kara Mikula) flutters about making bizarre predictions and demands.

With an energetic song called "Betsey Trotwood Copperfield/It's a Boy," Mikula kicks the story off with rousing energy and plenty of spunk. Then comes Young David (Calvin Knegten at the performance I saw), who shoulders much of the first act, with his older counterpart stepping in on occasion to make observations or lend reflective moral support. Young David will be bounced on his mother's knee, then he's off to a seaside village to visit with Peggotty's charming family, before being sent to a cruel boarding school at the behest of his vicious stepfather, Mr. Murdstone (Will Reynolds).

From there, the poor boy will be orphaned then forced into hard labor, before seeking salvation from the aforementioned aunt. Even as he finds relief from one vexation, other troubles arise. But so, too, do meaningful relationships with debonair classmate Steerforth (Owen Squire Smith), scrappy debtor Mr. Micawber (Andy Taylor), a befuddled but brilliant family friend Mr. Dick (Aaron Michael Ray), and his benevolent bestie Agnes Wickfield (Tatiana Wechsler).

Now, as the story roams from the Copperfield estate to Yarmouth, London, and beyond across decades, Copperfield! keeps its cast almost alarmingly small. Around Conte's strong central performance, 13 adult actors not only play several characters each, but also pick up instruments to join in the orchestration. In this way, they turn a tiny stage — enhanced by flanking platforms and a modest but efficient set design — into a wide world that is cruel, kind, and often wondrous.

Bobby Conte leads a marvelous ensemble cast in Copperfield!

Bobby Conte and Cathryn Wake in "Copperfield! The New Musical." Credit: Jeremy Daniel

As an admirer of Iannucci's adaptation of David Copperfield, I thought of it often while witnessing Copperfield! In that, I found a curious distinction. Iannucci focused on David's pursuit of identity, drawing attention to how he was forced to code-switch and even change his name depending on his company. Scharf's approach is less cerebral, focusing instead on the search for home.

The plays opening number "I Am Home" is reprised throughout the show, as David reflects on when he felt safe and thought his happy ending was assured. Of course, whether you know Dickens' work or just that this musical is unlikely to end in the first act, this reprise toys with our hearts, because we know there's more to come. While the company brings out guitars, trumpets, pianos, drums, and more instrumentation, Scharf's songs range from dastardly ("Mr. Murdstone Is the Master Now") to playful ("I Shake Your Hand Sir") to mournful ("Always the Pleasure Was Mine") to rollicking ("I Am a Rover"). In this, Scharf creates a thrilling odyssey in a simple setting, using his music and relying on this sensational cast to build David's world with their unbridled vigor.

Tatiana Wechsler, Aaron Michael Ray, Bobby Conte, and Kara Mikula in "Copperfield! The New Musical." Credit: Jeremy Daniel

While the whole of the cast sings their hearts out, there are a few standouts. Cormack, who plays Peggotty and Mrs. Micawber, among others, bursts with warmth and comedic bounciness, so her every entrance is a blessing. Mikula, as Aunt Betsey, is a deranged delight, singing with the trilling enthusiasm and the force of a windstorm, to the point where I yearned to see her tackle Sweeney Todd as Mrs. Lovett. Alexander Sovronsky awed me with the sheer breadth of his characters, playing the effusively warm Mr. Peggotty, the malevolent Mr. Creakle, the dignified then drunken Mr. Quinion, and a smattering of bit parts aside. With the toss of his lush silver hair and a switch up of his mouth, Sovronsky needs no costume change to change character. And in group numbers, he is so clearly enthralled, whether singing, dancing, or playing an instrument, that he's hard to look away from.

Conte is strong in the lead role, delivering nuance and heartache whether mourning over his mother or falling in love, not once but twice. Wechsler is lovely in the role of besotted Agnes, while Wake is hilarious and surprising as David's first love, Dora. Playing his mother, Wake is serene and oft on the verge of tears, bursting with motherly adoration of him. As his sweet, giddy bride, she is unhinged in the best way. Watching her throw herself into the Olympian vocal back-and-forth of their courtship ("Do-o-or-a, Part One and Part Two"), I was reminded of Jane Krakowski and her recent Broadway stint in Oh, Mary! In this latter role Wake is explosively hilarious, deepening my appreciation for the sincerity in her first-act performance of Clara.

With so much going on, Copperfield! is a whole lot of show. And admittedly, that means the third act drags as the eponymous hero catches up to the romantic realization even newcomers to the story will clock in Act II. Still, bursting with energy, passion, and jaw-dropping talent, Copperfield! The New Musical is a wonder not to be missed.

Copperfield! The New Musical is now playing through Dec. 6 in New York.

NASHVILLE — Country music star Trace Adkins has spent decades entertaining audiences around the world. But on Saturday morning in Tennessee, the roles were reversed, as the longtime outdoorsman found himself a little starstruck by some of the biggest names in professional bass fishing.

Adkins performed the national anthem before the championship round of World Bass Enterprises' inaugural The Champions tournament on Old Hickory Lake, where 15 of the world's top professional bass anglers were competing for a record $1.25 million first-place prize.

And while the country star was impressed by the talent on the water, he told OutKick OutDoors that he particularly appreciated the sport's willingness to embrace faith and patriotism.

"I'm just thrilled to be here today. Some of the finest anglers on the planet," Adkins said in an exclusive interview. "And, you know, growing up fishing, it's like these guys are rock stars, you know? And to get to shake hands with some of them.

"I like that they're not afraid of God or patriotism. They're going to pray and have the national anthem before they fish."

For Adkins, who grew up in Louisiana and has long been an avid outdoorsman, fishing is much more than a passing hobby.

In fact, he's been celebrating the sport for years.

His 2011 hit "Just Fishin'" tells the story of a father taking his young daughter fishing, capturing the idea that time spent together outdoors is about much more than what's on the end of the line.

That same year, Adkins opened the lake on his Middle Tennessee property to a group from Big Brothers Big Sisters, giving children and their mentors an opportunity to enjoy a day of fishing and other outdoor activities.

And in a 2017 interview with Fox News, the singer joked that he'd found a way to make his fishing trips considerably more productive.

"I don't go on fishing trips anymore," he said at the time. "I stocked my own hole full of fish so I don't fish, I just catch."

But even with his own stocked fishing hole, Adkins still appreciates the professionals who have helped turn bass fishing into a major spectator sport.

"Oh yeah, I grew up, there was Roland Martin and Bill Dance on television," Adkins said. "You know, they were the only two. When you look at it now, I mean, what it's become, it's just exciting."

And the scale of this weekend's event illustrated just how far the sport has come.

The Champions brought together top competitors from the Bassmaster Elite Series and Major League Fishing's Bass Pro Tour for a three-day competition featuring a $3.25 million total purse — the largest in professional bass fishing history.

The tournament concluded Saturday with Matt Robertson claiming the $1.25 million grand prize, capping off a historic weekend for professional bass fishing.

The event also featured a team competition between the two circuits, with Bassmaster's Team Stars winning The Champions Cup on Friday before the remaining 15 anglers advanced to Saturday's individual championship.

For Adkins, who has enjoyed fishing since childhood, being part of that moment meant getting to celebrate both the sport and the traditions he values.

5 habits that could help keep you out of a nursing home, from an orthopedic surgeon

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Three in four Americans aged 50 and older would like to remain in their homes as they age, according to the AARP — and preserving that independence starts with daily habits.

Dr. Vonda Wright, a double board-certified orthopedic surgeon, author and authority on active aging and mobility, who is based in Orlando, Florida, said there are three main problems that can make it difficult for older adults to age in place.

Those include difficulty getting up from a chair and caring for themselves, memory problems, and incontinence that makes it hard to manage personal hygiene.

FORGET THE GYM: A 30-SECOND TEST MAY BE ONE OF THE BEST INDICATORS OF LONGEVITY

"The missing piece in most conversations between mental health, physical health and independence is your skeletal longevity," Wright said.

Staying independent with age requires attention to the entire musculoskeletal system, including the muscles, bones and tendons, according to the surgeon.

Wright shared the following habits as most important for maintaining independence outside of a nursing home.

No. 1: Keep moving

The human body "craves mobility," Wright said.

"When you think about it, it's our first skill. We learn to walk when we're 1," she pointed out.

WEEKLY WEIGHTLIFTING SWEET SPOT MAY BE LINKED TO LONGER LIFE, STUDY FINDS

For older adults, staying active can help preserve the ability to handle everyday tasks independently. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention suggests low-impact activities such as walking, cycling and water exercise.

"Even when your hips hurt, even when your knees hurt, you should move on the ground, or get in a pool in chest-high water," Wright suggested.

While the doctor does not recommend exercising through a new injury or severe pain, she pushed back against the belief that rest is the best way to address pain.

PEOPLE LIVING PAST 100 IN ONE TINY VILLAGE SHARE 3 DAILY HABITS

"Rest doesn't cure anything. When you have, for instance, arthritic knees, do you know what can help? Building the muscles around your knees, building the muscles in your hips to support your knees," she said. "So rest is not the answer for feeling better — staying strong and mobile is."

No. 2: Build muscle

Strength training can help older adults maintain the ability to get out of a chair, climb stairs and carry groceries — everyday tasks that support independent living.

There is never an age or skill level when it’s too late to build muscle, according to Wright.

FITNESS EXPERT REVEALS 6 THINGS PEOPLE OVER 35 SHOULD DO AFTER WORKOUTS TO STAY STRONG

"Even if you feel like you've declined or your parents have declined, there is no age or skill level when we can't become more mobile, when we can't build muscle or reconnect the neuromuscular pathways," she said.

It’s even possible to build muscle sitting in a wheelchair, she noted. "Some of the very first research done by Dr. Maria Fiatarone at Tufts University showed that even at 90 years old, you can regain function."

In that small 1990 study, nine nursing home residents who completed eight weeks of high-intensity resistance training increased their muscle strength by an average of 174%, and also showed improvements in muscle size and mobility.

The National Institute on Aging recommends that older adults perform muscle-strengthening activities at least two days a week, targeting all major muscle groups, with recovery time between sessions.

Some options include resistance bands, hand weights and body-weight exercises. Beginners are advised to start gradually and seek guidance from a doctor or physical therapist as needed.

No. 3: Eat enough to support strength

Older adults tend to eat less, which can undermine efforts to maintain energy and build muscle, Wright warned.

"It’s something I see even happening to my 86-year-old parents; they're just eating less and less," she said. "And we cannot fuel the muscle we want to build [and] the energy we need without a protein-forward, adequate-calorie diet."

Although older adults may require fewer calories, they still need enough nutrients to support their health, according to the National Institute on Aging.

To help maintain muscle, the agency recommends that older adults consume protein-rich foods throughout the day, including seafood, dairy, fortified soy products, beans and lentils. Specific calorie needs will vary based on each person’s weight and activity level.

No. 4: Drop the mindset that decline is inevitable

Wright urged older adults to challenge assumptions about what they can and can’t do as they age.

"The most common mistake I see seniors making as they age is assuming that aging is an inevitable decline from vitality to frailty," she said.

"They just assume that a certain number of birthday candles predisposes them to sitting on a couch the rest of their life. The reality is that there is no age or skill level when you're done."

No. 5: Have open conversations

Wright suggested that people should start working toward future independence during their busy working years.

"We need to start thinking about that in our busiest times of life, in our 30s, our 40s, our prime times of working," she said. "It’s part of a long-term, lifetime plan to live where you want to live, with whom you want to live, when you want to live there."

The doctor recommends that adults directly ask their aging parents what assistance they need and to explore available support, including community programs.

In many cases, she said, parents do not want to be a burden to their adult children, so they never ask for anything.

"I think this is a time for really open, frank conversations," Wright said. "What do you want help with? How can I help you? How could we get help for you if possible?"

As an example, Wright said her elderly parents live with her family. When she noticed their meals started to suffer while she was traveling, she arranged for support to help them continue managing their lives.

"I have half of their meals delivered every week because I'm not going to be home to shop or cook," she said. "They remain independent, but with assistance. And that's what works for us."

Americans forced to change clocks again as Senate stalls Trump-backed daylight saving time bill

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Daylight saving time has dodged the wrath of President Donald Trump — for now.

As Americans again participate in the twice-yearly ritual of fiddling with their clocks — this time rolling back an hour on Sunday morning — Congress has fallen short of tackling the issue in time.

Earlier this year, the House passed legislation that would allow states to voluntarily observe daylight saving time year-round. But progress on the legislation has ground to a halt in the Senate, even though it’s a popular issue with voters and Trump.

BATTLE OVER DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME ERUPTS AS PUSH TO STOP CHANGING CLOCKS HITS SENATE WALL

But it’s not for lack of trying. Trump has routinely pressured the upper chamber to pass the bill and re-upped that call earlier this week in the midst of traveling across the country to rally in key states and safe red bastions to urge voters to keep Republicans in power.

"The United States Senate is letting the daylight saving bill die when a vast Majority of the Country want it," Trump said on Truth Social. "Most people are demanding light in the evening, but ALL want not to change their clocks anymore. TAKE A VOTE!"

Trump wants the Senate to pass, or at least take a vote on, the Sunshine Protection Act, but it’s facing heavy headwinds from a broad swathe of lawmakers in the upper chamber on both sides of the aisle.

TRUMP FACES REPUBLICAN REBELLION OVER DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME AS SENATE BILL NEARS DEAD END

His pressure campaign got to the point that he shared the cellphone number of one of his top allies, Sen. Tom Cotton, R-Ark., one of the more vocal opponents of the bill, urging supporters to call the lawmaker and demand he work on passing the bill.

"No one likes changing the clock, me included, but Congress has enacted permanent Daylight Saving Time multiple times before and repealed it each time once Americans experienced absurdly late sunrises in the winter," Cotton said in response. "Arkansans don’t want their kids going to school in the pitch dark, and I’ll always put Arkansas first."

The House of Representatives passed the Sunshine Protection Act along bipartisan lines in July. Majorities of Democrats and Republicans voted in favor of the bill, which passed 307 to 117. Just 22 Republicans and 95 Democrats opposed it.

But it’s one of several pieces of legislation that House lawmakers complain has fallen by the wayside in the upper chamber. House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, R-La., pressed the Senate to take up that bill in comments to Fox News Digital back in early October.

Scalise told Fox News Digital, "There's no reason Americans should lose an hour of sunlight at the end of the day when more daylight means more time with our families, more activity for our businesses and safer communities."

"President Trump has been pushing to make daylight saving time permanent, and House Republicans delivered by overwhelmingly passing the Sunshine Protection Act. It's a commonsense change and the Senate should take it up," Scalise said.
