Considering a formal apology doesn’t mean accepting that British people now should accept the financial liability for any party and things that happened hundreds of years ago, in which they had no role.

For almost 250 years (1560s-1807), British ships trafficked more than three million enslaved African men, women and children in a state policy backed by the monarchy and parliament.

There were still 800,000 enslaved people on British plantations when enslavement was finally abolished by parliament in 1833

Britain has never paid any compensation or reparations to the people who were enslaved, or their countries.
